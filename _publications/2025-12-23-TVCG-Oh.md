We present ForceCtrl, a novel hand-raycasting technique designed to promote Control-Display (CD) gain-based precise pointing in 3D by utilizing surface Electromyography (sEMG) as a precision control cue. Unlike previous target-dependent approaches to enhance pointing precision, we allow users to aim at an arbitrary point with high precision, overcoming insufficient human motor skills in controlling pointing. Moreover, we employ a user-adaptive force-integrated pinch gesture to turn on and off the CD gain with ease. We form a classification model that estimates the user’s pinch strength from the sEMG signal based on observations on the behavior of subjective force exertion. In our work, we introduce three different CD-gain-based pointing strategies and perform a comparison with the baseline ray pointing technique through target selection and placement tasks. The results reveal that employing CD gain to the ray pointing decreases selection errors, pointing jitters, and participants’ workloads, especially for distant targets. Our research highlights the benefits of applying CD gain to the hand-raycasting technique for scenes with small and densely packed targets and showcases the potential use of force-integrated pinch gesture in 3D hand interaction models.
